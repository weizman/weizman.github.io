Vulnerability Disclosure Policy

My name is Gal Weizman (weizmangal@gmail.com).
If I disclosed a security vulnerability/flaw to your organization, I may have referenced this document.
If so, the disclosure process will follow the policy described here.

Timeline

The reported finding is subject to a classic 90-day disclosure deadline.
From the moment the vendor officially considers the issue addressed and fixed, I will grant a 14-day grace period before making the findings public.
I will consider granting an additional 14-day grace period upon request if shipping the fix to affected users is complicated and requires more time.
If the flaw was or is being exploited in the wild, the window will be shortened to 7 days to help defend users.
If the flaw remains unaddressed when the 90-day deadline arrives, the findings will still be published.
The vendor already knowing of the disclosed vulnerability, whether internally or by an external report, will not affect my right to publicly share my research and findings.
The disclosure window is prone to being shortened in the following exceptions:

  • 1. Any lack of response or engagement throughout the process for 20 days or more on behalf of the vendor. This includes no response to the original disclosure report;
  • 2. The vendor's decision not to fix the issue for any reason.
In those cases, the findings will be published immediately, without waiting for the full 90-day window.

Publishing

The findings will be published in formats such as a written blog post, PoC video, IoCs, vulnerability and exploit code artifacts, conferences, and more.
The vendor may request an opportunity to review these artifacts before they are made public; in that case, the vendor will have 3 business days to provide feedback.
I may reject any feedback.
That being said, as with any previous work of mine, I am fully committed to communicating my findings positively and never casting an unjustified negative light on the vendor.

Good faith

Eventually, I come in good faith.
I am committed to the best, most efficient and most responsible disclosure process and will engage with the affected vendor as soon as possible, in any way possible.
I am committed to always helping with clarification and remediation proposals.
I will always communicate positively, with the objective of helping the vendor address the disclosed vulnerability as a first priority. Anything about this policy can be revisited on a per-case basis, subject to my decision only.

Acknoledgment

When it makes sense, I would expect a CVE to be assigned.
If that's the case and the vendor refuses to assign one, I reserve the right to approach a CNA myself to address that.
I would appreciate being acknowledged as Gal Weizman as the reporter of the vulnerability if you decide to share the finding yourself. This is regardless of any content I will publish.
A bounty reward is very appreciated. Ideally, based on the vendor's vulnerability disclosure program, if one already exists.
The decision to reward such a bounty and its amounts will be communicated in the published content. Readers of my content usually think kindly of vendors that provide adequate rewards for my research.
